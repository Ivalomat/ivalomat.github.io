Integration of Active Learning with Molecular Docking for Efficient Drug Discovery in Natural Product Databases

This research project was originally developed as an undergraduate thesis. Due to time constraints, I ultimately completed my degree through a different graduation modality. The computational work and manuscript were completed, but the document did not undergo the final formal review or defense process.

The project investigated whether active learning could reduce the number of molecular docking calculations needed to identify promising natural products. A machine-learning-guided virtual-screening workflow was applied to compounds from the COCONUT database, using Vina-GPU docking results as the regression target for iterative compound prioritization.

Project Components

Data Preparation and Molecular Docking

Curation and preprocessing of natural products from COCONUT, generation of three-dimensional structures, receptor preparation and GPU-accelerated molecular docking with Vina-GPU against Mycobacterium tuberculosis thymidylate kinase.

Molecular Representations and Models

Comparison of Morgan and MAP4 fingerprints and Mordred descriptors with Random Forest, Support Vector Regression and Multiple Linear Regression models for docking-score prediction.

Active Learning Workflow

Iterative greedy selection was used to prioritize predicted high-affinity compounds, dock each selected batch, expand the training set and retrain the predictive model. Performance was compared with random selection and exhaustive docking baselines.

Evaluation and Main Findings

Recovery of the top one percent of compounds was analyzed as a function of the number of molecules explored and runtime. Random Forest produced rapid enrichment, SVR achieved competitive recovery with greater computational cost, and MLR was faster but generally less effective at recovering top compounds.

Bravais Net: An Augmented Reality Learning App for Bravais Lattices

Bravais Net is an Android augmented reality application developed with Unity and Vuforia Engine to support the study of crystallographic lattice systems. By placing the device camera over a printed image-target card, the application recognizes the marker and displays the corresponding three-dimensional lattice model together with its main structural characteristics.

The physical deck represents the fourteen Bravais lattices. Each card identifies the lattice type, provides an example of an element or compound with that structural arrangement and contains the visual target used by the application. The card colors distinguish the centering type: red for primitive, blue for body-centered, green for face-centered and purple for base-centered lattices.

Project Components

Android Augmented Reality App

A mobile application created in Unity and integrated with Vuforia Engine for camera-based recognition of printed image targets and real-time placement of crystallographic models.

Interactive 3D Visualization

The recognized card activates a three-dimensional representation of the lattice and displays information such as its crystal system, centering and lattice parameters directly within the augmented scene.

Printable Educational Card Deck

The printable template includes cards for the fourteen Bravais lattices. Each card combines a recognizable visual marker, the lattice classification and an example of a real material associated with that structural arrangement.

Scientific Learning Experience

The project connects physical learning materials with interactive visualization, helping students relate abstract crystallographic classifications to spatial models and examples of real elements and compounds.

Demonstration Video

The demonstration is narrated in Spanish. An English subtitle track is included and can be enabled or disabled through the video controls.

First-Principles Benchmarking of Bulk Platinum with CP2K

This training project was completed during a three-month research stay at Ruhr University Bochum as part of the RESOLV Undergraduate Exchange Program. Under the supervision of Dr. Chanbum Park in the research group of Prof. Dominik Marx, the work focused on learning periodic density functional theory workflows and becoming familiar with CP2K/QUICKSTEP.

The attached report and presentation were prepared under the broader working title Deciphering the Platinum Electrolyte Interface in Aqueous Solution. The completed stage documented here concentrates on validating the computational description of bulk face-centered cubic platinum as a preparatory step toward more complex simulations of platinum-electrolyte interfaces. The results were presented internally to the research group at the conclusion of the stay.

Project Components

CP2K Training and Computational Setup

Construction and simulation of a periodic 48-atom fcc platinum model using CP2K/QUICKSTEP, Goedecker-Teter-Hutter pseudopotentials and double-zeta Gaussian basis sets.

Convergence and Functional Testing

Evaluation of plane-wave cutoff convergence and comparison of PBE, RPBE and dispersion-corrected treatments to understand their effect on the calculated properties of platinum.

Structural and Cohesive Properties

Determination of equilibrium lattice parameters and cohesive energies from total-energy calculations, followed by comparison with experimental data and previous density functional theory results.

Equation of State and Bulk Modulus

Fitting of energy-volume data with the Birch-Murnaghan equation of state to estimate the bulk modulus and assess the mechanical response of bulk platinum.

Selected Results

Depending on the exchange-correlation and dispersion treatment, the calculated equilibrium lattice parameter was approximately 3.94-3.99 Å and the estimated bulk modulus was approximately 237-259 GPa. These results were compared with the reported experimental values of 3.92 Å and 278 GPa.

Electroncito: An Educational Mobile Game for Electron Counting

Electroncito is an Android educational game developed with MIT App Inventor as the final project for an Organometallic Chemistry course. The application was designed to reinforce valence-electron counting in coordination complexes, particularly the use of the 18-electron rule as a practical guideline in organometallic chemistry.

The game uses a multiplayer guessing format. One participant holds the device against their forehead while the other players view a coordination structure displayed on the screen and provide clues. The participant must determine the electron count before the timer expires. Correct answers allow the group to advance to another structure, while missed rounds can activate a playful penalty section.

Project Components

Organometallic Chemistry Practice

Coordination structures are used as timed exercises for applying electron-counting methods and discussing the 18-electron rule, including the contribution of the metal, ligands and the overall charge of the complex.

Multiplayer Game Mechanics

Players complete five timed rounds. Device movement advances to a new structure, difficult questions can be skipped by touching the screen, and the final score or penalty screen depends on the number of missed rounds.

MIT App Inventor Development

The application was implemented through MIT App Inventor using visual interface design and block-based programming to manage screens, timers, sounds, scoring, structure selection and device-motion events.

User Testing and Iterative Improvement

Two evaluations with chemistry students and professors were used to refine the interface and gameplay. Feedback led to clearer penalties, a shorter response time, background music and sound effects in the revised version.

Evaluation and Publication

The first classroom evaluation involved 30 participants and produced an average rating of 8.20 out of 10. After the application was revised, a second group of 27 participants evaluated the new version, which received an average rating of 9.89 out of 10. The design, implementation and evaluation were subsequently documented in a Spanish-language article published in the Journal of Basic Sciences.

Founding and Leading the ACS-UJAT Student Chapter

This project represents the founding and first year of the American Chemical Society Student Chapter at the Universidad Juarez Autonoma de Tabasco. As founder and inaugural president, I worked alongside Vice President Yohali Abigail Espinosa Vallarta and our faculty advisor, Dr. Carlos Ernesto Lobato Garcia, to build a student-led platform that connected chemistry students, professors, ACS resources and external academic and industrial opportunities.

The chapter combined scientific outreach with community building. Its activities included welcome and engagement events for Chemistry students, a symbolic closing and leadership handover to the following generation, an academic website with a faculty directory and student-support resources, a conversation with Latin American graduate students at Cornell University, and group visits to the Laguna Verde nuclear power plant and the La Gloria sugar mill.

Leadership and Community Initiatives

Chapter Founding and Continuity

Establishment of the ACS-UJAT Student Chapter, coordination of its inaugural leadership team and development of activities intended to strengthen collaboration between students, professors and the wider chemistry community.

Student Welcome and Community Events

Organization of Chemistry-program welcome and engagement events, participation in National Chemistry Day, and preparation of a final gathering that combined a farewell with the symbolic handover to the chapter's next generation.

Academic Website and International Outreach

Development of a chapter website containing leadership information, a faculty directory and free academic resources, together with a conversation on studying abroad led by Latin American graduate students at Cornell University.

Scientific and Industrial Visits

Coordination of a group visit to the Laguna Verde nuclear power plant and the La Gloria sugar mill, giving participating students direct exposure to industrial environments and practical applications of chemistry.

ACS Latin America Global Leadership Summit

The chapter's leadership work led to our selection and financial support to participate in the 2024 ACS Latin America Global Leadership Summit in Bogota, Colombia. The summit, held from November 7 to 9, provided an opportunity to present the chapter's first-year activities, reflect openly on its results and challenges, and exchange experiences with other chemistry leaders from across Latin America.

Student Leadership, Support and University Governance at DACB

My work in the student board of the Division Academica de Ciencias Basicas began in May 2023, when I joined SADACB as secretary. In October 2024, I assumed its presidency and continued in that role until September 2025. Across both positions, I helped coordinate student services, community activities and institutional representation for students from the division's different academic programs.

After becoming president, I also served as a board member of the Consejo Estudiantil Universitario del Estado de Tabasco (CDEUT) and as a student representative in the Honorable University Council of UJAT. This work expanded my responsibilities from local student support to university-level governance, where I participated in discussions and decisions concerning internal regulations, the creation or modification of degree programs, postgraduate programs and specializations, and other institutional matters affecting the university community.

University Governance and Representation

Leadership Progression
